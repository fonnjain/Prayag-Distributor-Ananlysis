---
name: Festival seasonality decision
description: Confirmed treatment of Diwali-aligned seasonality evidence and the resulting forecasting policy.
---

Use a fixed calendar-month curve for company-level seasonality. Do not replace it with a Diwali-relative curve.

**Why:** The confirmed territory-register analysis did not establish a recurring Diwali-relative curve. One clean FY2024–25 window has a local pre-festival rise followed by a clean post-festival slump, but its festival week is month-end contaminated. FY2025–26's positive relative weeks also collide with month-end, while FY2023–24 has no invoice dates and FY2022–23 is unavailable. That is insufficient and contaminated evidence, not evidence that a festival effect is absent. March remained the company, PTMT, SWR, and Sanitaryware peak; CP moved contrary to the Diwali-date hypothesis. CPVC, CP, and Sanitaryware have materially different monthly shapes, so a company curve is not a segment forecast.

**How to apply:** Keep the current company monthly curve for broad company planning and explain its scope. When a feature forecasts or compares individual CPVC, CP, or Sanitaryware performance, prefer that segment's own curve rather than inheriting the company allocation. Treat within-month month-end timing as a separate concern from monthly seasonality. For a future clean test, preserve CRM order-created/placed and approval timestamps alongside invoice dates, and classify any festival-relative window crossing month-end as contaminated rather than negative evidence.
