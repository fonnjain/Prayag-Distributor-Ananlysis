---
name: Festival seasonality decision
description: Confirmed treatment of Diwali-aligned seasonality evidence and the resulting forecasting policy.
---

Use a fixed calendar-month curve for company-level seasonality. Do not replace it with a Diwali-relative curve.

**Why:** The confirmed territory-register analysis did not establish a recurring pre-festival build: the one usable surrounding sequence is contaminated for its festival week, while FY2025–26's positive relative weeks collide with month-end; FY2023–24 has no invoice dates and FY2022–23 is unavailable. That is insufficient evidence, not evidence that a build is absent. Separately, the festival-week dip is consistent across both dated years in PTMT, CP, Sanitaryware, and CPVC; SWR behaves differently and pulls up the company aggregate. March remained the company, PTMT, SWR, and Sanitaryware peak; CP moved contrary to the Diwali-date hypothesis. CPVC, CP, and Sanitaryware have materially different monthly shapes, so a company curve is not a segment forecast.

**How to apply:** Keep the current company monthly curve for broad company planning and explain its scope. When a feature forecasts or compares individual CPVC, CP, or Sanitaryware performance, prefer that segment's own curve rather than inheriting the company allocation. Treat within-month month-end timing as a separate concern from monthly seasonality. A week containing a major festival can be expected to run roughly 30–70% below normal in household segments; this belongs as interpretive context on Momentum, not as a festival forecast curve or performance alert. For a future clean test, preserve CRM order-created/placed and approval timestamps alongside invoice dates, and classify any festival-relative window crossing month-end as contaminated rather than negative evidence.
