## The finding that reorders everything

August sits in a state nobody designed:

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Calendar-frozen | **Yes** — 7 Sep 05:30 IST |
| Persistently anchored | **No** — `frozen_at` is NULL |
| Ordinary replacement | **Blocked** by the strict shrink guard |
| Frozen-drift eligibility | **No** — requires a persisted frozen month |

The sequence: last successful replacement 5 September at 12,887 rows. At the
freeze transition the source had fallen to 12,871, and the strict guard rejected
the lower read — **correctly**. But that rejection meant August was never
anchored.

So August is frozen by the calendar and **invisible to every repair path**. That
is not a defect in any single guard; it is a gap between two of them, and it will
catch September next unless closed.

## Three corrections accepted

**The 492 rows cannot be verified.** The 2 September run kept only the count, not
the rows or a fingerprint, and later replacement deleted that version. The
serial-range candidate is a hypothesis, not a figure. **This is exactly why the
ledger goes first.**

**Detection is not read-only** — it inserts drift-check records. Right not to have
run it.

**There is no two-person approval primitive.** The guarded apply call with preview
hash, operator and reason *is* the approval. My prompt implied a gate that does
not exist.
