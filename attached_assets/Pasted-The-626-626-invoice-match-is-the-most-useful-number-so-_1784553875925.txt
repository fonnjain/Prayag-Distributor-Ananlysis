The 626 = 626 invoice match is the most useful number so far. It rules out
whole-invoice deletion. Now settle the line-level question.

THE DECISIVE TEST - INVOICE TOTALS

Line splitting can change how an invoice is broken into rows. It cannot change
the invoice's total value. So:

For each of the 626 Jul-26 invoices, compare SUM(amount) in the DB against
SUM(Taxable Value) in the sheet. Bucket them:

  exact match          - line splitting differs, no data missing
  DB higher            - duplicates, or rows removed from the sheet
  sheet higher         - the DB is missing rows

Report the count and total value in each bucket, plus the ten largest
discrepancies in each direction with invoice number, DB total, sheet total and
difference.

If most invoices match exactly and a handful differ wildly, the problem is
localised. If nearly every invoice shows the DB roughly double, it is
systematic duplication and nothing was deleted at all.

Then pick ONE invoice where the DB has materially more rows than the sheet and
print BOTH sets side by side, every column. Looking at twenty rows will show
in seconds what the aggregates cannot.

THE OTHER FINDING - COLOUR IS NOT STORED

You confirmed color does not exist in sale_line. But SALE SHEET 26-27 HAS a
Color column at position 9 - I read the header directly:

  Serial no, Invoice No, Date, Bill From, Customer.Name, City, Destination,
  Item Code, Color, Quantity, Mrp, Sale Rate, Taxable Value, GROUP, STATION,
  STATE, STATE HEAD, MONTH

So colour is read and discarded at load.

That is exactly why serial_no became the dedup key. The documented case:
invoice 12600285, item 188, qty 88, Rs.25,867 - serial 1802 WHITE, serial 1803
IVORY, two genuinely different products identical on every other field.

Consequences:
  a) The DB cannot distinguish colour variants at all.
  b) Your duplicate analysis is understated - two colour variants collapse into
     one distinct combination, so 5,575 is a floor, not the true count.
  c) The key had to use serial_no
