**Date:** 2 September 2026

**Standing rule:** every figure states which database or source it came from.

**Decision taken:** month closure is **IST midnight**. This is an Indian sales
operation working on IST data, and the business day ends at IST midnight.

## The defect

```js
return nowMs > Date.UTC(calYear, calMonth + 1, 0);
```

`Date.UTC(year, month+1, 0)` is midnight at the **start** of the month's final
calendar day. August 2026 closed at **31 August 00:00:00.001 UTC** — that is
**05:30 IST** — with the whole of 31 August still to trade.

| Boundary | UTC | IST | vs current |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current | 31 Aug 00:00:00.001 | 31 Aug 05:30 | — |
| **IST midnight (chosen)** | **31 Aug 18:30** | **1 Sep 00:00** | **18.5 h later** |
| UTC month end | 1 Sep 00:00 | 1 Sep 05:30 | 24 h later |

Months currently close **18.5 hours early**.

## Section B — the code change, after Section A

```
Code change. Move month closure to IST midnight. Three helpers, nothing else.

Replace:

  return nowMs > Date.UTC(calYear, calMonth + 1, 0);

with a boundary at IST midnight following the month's last day - 00:00 IST on
the 1st of the next month, which is 18:30 UTC on the last day.

Make the IST intent EXPLICIT in the code. A named constant and a comment saying
"IST midnight, UTC+05:30" - not a bare arithmetic offset. A stray
`- 5.5 * 60 * 60 * 1000` in a date expression is exactly what gets "simplified"
later by someone who does not know why it is there.

Apply to the three CLOSURE occurrences only.

Do NOT touch the primary-velocity days-in-month calculation or the general
analytics month-end date calculation. They use the same expression to mean "the
last day number of the month", which is correct there. Changing them breaks both.

Also do NOT:
  - change the V4 arrears guard or its 15% threshold
  - change the Prompt 43 persistence fix, which is live and working
```

## Notes

- A4 is the one worth having in writing. If August would have stored correctly
  under the IST rule, then the closure defect is what let this through and the
  arrears fix was the backstop. Both were worth fixing; knowing the order matters
  for anyone reading this later.
- A5 is the only real risk in the change. Anything running on the last day of the
  month expecting closure now runs 18.5 hours before it is true.
- A6 is not part of this change. Seven genuine A1 alerts have been suppressed
  since 31 August by false ones. They should be looked at by a person, and the
  list is easier to get now than later.

  codebase already carries five disagreeing state vocabularies from exactly that
  kind of undocumented decision.
