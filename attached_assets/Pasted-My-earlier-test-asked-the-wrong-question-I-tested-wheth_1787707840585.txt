My earlier test asked the wrong question. I tested whether the ANNUAL PEAK
moves with Diwali - it does not, March wins every year. But that is not what
was being asked.

The real question: is there a LOCAL pattern around Diwali - sales building in
the weeks BEFORE, then dipping during the festival period - regardless of
where the annual peak sits?

Both can be true. March can be the year's peak and October-November can still
show a build-then-dip.

RUN THIS

Diwali: 24 Oct 2022 · 12 Nov 2023 · 1 Nov 2024 · 20 Oct 2025

For each year, print territory net by WEEK relative to Diwali:
    weeks -6, -5, -4, -3, -2, -1, 0 (festival week), +1, +2, +3

Express each week as a MULTIPLE of that year's average territory week, so the
years are comparable and the annual trend is removed. A week at 1.30 is 30%
above that year's normal; 0.70 is 30% below.

What I expect if the pattern is real:
  weeks -4 to -1   above 1.0, rising
  week 0           BELOW 1.0 - shops closed, transport stopped, holidays
  weeks +1 to +2   below or near 1.0 - the pipeline was filled beforehand

THREE THINGS TO GET RIGHT

1. Use the FESTIVAL WEEK ITSELF as week 0, not the calendar month. Diwali is a
   five-day festival, so include roughly Dhanteras through Bhai Dooj.

2. Do it by SEGMENT as well as company. Sanitaryware and PTMT are household
   goods bought for home completion before Diwali. CPVC and SWR are
   construction materials and may not follow it at all.

3. Given the month-end finding, a Diwali falling near month-end will collide
   with the month-end spike. Note where each Diwali sits in its month:
     24 Oct 2022 - late month
     12 Nov 2023 - mid month
     1 Nov 2024  - month start
     20 Oct 2025 - late month
   The 2023 and 2024 dates are cleanest for isolating the festival effect,
   because they do not overlap the month-end concentration.

ALSO PRINT
The same for Holi - 8 Mar 2023, 25 Mar 2024, 14 Mar 2025 - though March is
already the peak month and the financial year-end effect may drown it.

WHY THIS MATTERS NOW
Diwali 2026 is 8 November. The build lands in OCTOBER and the dip in NOVEMBER.
Last year Diwali was 20 October, so the build was in SEPTEMBER and the dip in
OCTOBER. Same two months, opposite roles - and a like-month comparison across
those will read as performance when it is timing.
