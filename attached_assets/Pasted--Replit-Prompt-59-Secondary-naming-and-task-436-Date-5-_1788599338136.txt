## Section A — "secondary" means order booking. Say so.

The audit established this and it is not in dispute. `secondary_register_line`
and `secondary_sku_line` are loaded from the **same workbook and same tab** that
`mgmt_sources.json` configures as the order-booking source — exact ID and tab
match for FY2024-25 and FY2025-26.

The schema settles it: Order ID, Order Value, Sub Total, Order Total, Cat. No.
**No invoice, no challan, no dispatch document, no delivery confirmation, no
receipt field.**

There is **no line-level secondary sale source anywhere in the system.**

## Section B — task #436, scoping only

The frozen tables hold month labels derived from the misparsed dates. FY2025-26
proves it: **identical 379,439 rows and ₹2,310,913,869 annually**, but Q3
overstated by ₹5.83 Cr and Q4 understated by ₹5.58 Cr against the corrected
source.

**There may be a cheaper answer than rebuilding.**

```
Read-only and scoping only. Build nothing, replace nothing, unfreeze nothing.

Two options. I want both costed before choosing.

OPTION 1 - DISCLOSE

Leave the tables as they are. Label the affected months on the surfaces that
read them.

B1. Which surfaces read FY2024-25 or FY2025-26 MONTHLY figures from these
    tables? The audit named SKU Deep Dive, Secondary Performance's distributor
    tab, Red Alert B3 retailer, the push list, breadth trajectory and AI
    Growth activation. Confirm the complete list.

B2. What would a disclosure look like on each - a banner, a per-row marker, a
    footnote? Something like "monthly figures for this year use a superseded
    date basis; annual totals are correct."

B3. Can the disclosure be DERIVED rather than hardcoded - a flag on the FY, or
    a comparison against the live source? A hardcoded year list would go stale
    if the tables were ever rebuilt.

B4. Does anything COMPUTE on these months rather than display them - B3's
    prior-month comparison, breadth trajectory, the push engine? A wrong month
    that only displays is one thing; a wrong month that fires an alert is
    another. Name every computing consumer.

B5. Estimate the work.

OPTION 2 - REBUILD

B6. The audit said no safe path exists: secondary_register_line is append-only
    with month_label inside line_uid, secondary_sku_line has FY replacement for
    itself only, no transaction covers both, and both years are under
    frozen_registers.

    Given the FY2024-25 order load just built exactly this pattern - dedicated
    route, separate anchor file, staged validation, atomic replace, protected
    count verification, rollback - how much of that is reusable here?

B7. What would the controls be? For the order load they were rows, orders and
    value. Here the annual totals ALREADY match, so a control on annual
    figures would pass while proving nothing. The control has to be
    month-by-month against the corrected source.

B8. Both tables must move together or the register and SKU views would
    disagree. Is a single transaction across both feasible?

B9. What invalidates afterwards - snapshots, caches, alert history, AI
    artefacts? The audit mentioned rerunning dependent alert calculations.

B10. Estimate the work honestly, and name the risky parts.

THEN

B11. Recommend one, with reasons. Consider that FY2024-25 is described as
     rarely used, FY2025-26 is the active comparison year, and annual figures

Report only. Choose nothing, build nothing.
```

## Notes

- **Section A is cheap and should go first.** It changes no figure and removes a
  misreading that has already caught both of us. Someone will otherwise build a
  decision on "sell-through" that does not exist.
- **B4 is the question that decides Option 1's viability.** A wrong month that
  only displays can be labelled. A wrong month that feeds Red Alert B3's
  prior-period comparison produces false alerts, and no disclosure fixes that.
- **B7 matters.** The annual totals already reconcile, so any control built on
  them would pass while proving nothing. The FY2024-25 order load succeeded
  partly because its controls were genuinely discriminating.
- **B11 should weigh usage.** FY2024-25 is rarely used and FY2025-26 is the
  active comparison year. It may be worth rebuilding one and disclosing the
  other.

## Separately — 7 September, two days away

`isMonthFrozen` is a clock, not a job. At **00:00 UTC on the 7th — 05:30 IST** it
starts returning true for `Aug-26`, and `openMonthLabels` stops including it.
Nothing runs; August simply drops out of scope.

`secondary_sku_line` holds **zero August rows in production**. The 8,602 rows are
development only, and the Product-Wise file went into `secondary_order_line`.

So August freezes empty, and every SKU and sell-through surface reads it as a
measured zero — including **Red Alert B3's retailer branch**, which compares
current against prior months and would show every retailer as having stopped.

`SHORT_READ_TOLERANCE` at 0.98 guards a shrink, not an absence. A month never
loaded does not trip it.

The real fix is the August SKU export from Prayag. Failing that, a decision
before the 7th on whether August should be treated as unloaded rather than zero.
