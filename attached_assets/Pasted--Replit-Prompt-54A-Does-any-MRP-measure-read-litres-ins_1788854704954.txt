# Replit Prompt 54A — Does any MRP measure read litres instead of pieces?

**Date:** 3 September 2026
**Scope:** Read-only. Change nothing, add no conversion, alter no query.

**Standing rule:** every figure states which database or source it came from.

## Why this matters

`tankResolution.ts` is documented as the single source of truth for litres per
tank. It holds a canonical size map, two resolution routes, an ingest assertion
`assertTankQtyLtr`, and a comment recording that the 300-litre mapping was once
wrong and was corrected.

The Products export does not call it. It reports raw litres and warns about that
in a footnote. The consequence is measurable: WT-3LL-10 shows a **100% discount**
where the true figure is **68.1%** — 1,757,000 litres is 1,757 tanks at ₹5,103
against a ₹16,000 MRP.

Correctly converted, water tanks turn out to be the **worst-realising group in the
business at 70.6%**, against Sanitaryware's 56.2%. That is a commercial finding
that has been hidden behind a units artefact.

## What the order-booking load just established

`secondary_order_line.qty` records tanks in **pieces** — 7,813 rows, 26,703 total,
3.4 per line. As litres that would be impossible; as pieces it is normal.

So the same product family is recorded in **litres in `sale_line`** and **pieces in
`secondary_order_line`**, and neither table carries a unit column. That is not a
defect, but it is a trap for anything comparing the two.

## Notes

- **Question 3 is the whole prompt.** One export reporting litres is a display
  problem. A scheme slab, a price shrinker or a Red Alert guard reading litres
  would compute a 100% discount on every water tank and act on it. I asked for
  the rupee value flowing through each so the answer is sized, not just listed.
- **Question 7 is new**, and it follows from yesterday's load. Two tables, same
  products, different units, no unit column in either. Nothing may compare them
  today — but it is the kind of thing that gets built later by someone who
  reasonably assumes quantity means quantity.
- **Question 5 connects to an open Prayag query.** WCT codes sell ₹92.3 lakh with
  no master record at all, and they are part of the 780 unpriced codes worth
  ₹9.64 crore in query A8.
