## Notes

- **B3 is the one to get right.** Both legacy workbooks span twenty-three months.
  A load that trusts the workbook boundary inflates every prior year in the same
  direction, and the error would look like growth. B3.2 is the subtler half — the
  out-of-period tails may overlap each other.
- **B2 deserves a real answer rather than a reflex.** One legacy era surfaces a
  duplicate; two hide it. That asymmetry is the argument.
- **B7 matters because the instinct will be to fix them.** Nine exceptions across
  838,021 rows is an excellent source, and those nine are evidence. Recomputing
  them from the formula would make the data look cleaner and tell you less.
- **B9 is worth a moment.** The Retailer filter would carry tens of thousands of
  options on a page currently serving an empty table.
