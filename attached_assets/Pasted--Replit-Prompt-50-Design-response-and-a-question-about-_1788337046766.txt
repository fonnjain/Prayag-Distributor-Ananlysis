## Notes

- **A1 is the whole of Section A.** If the detection rule is "reinterpret until it
  falls inside the FY", it cannot distinguish a transposed date from a genuinely
  out-of-period one, and it will silently absorb both. That is a rule that always
  succeeds, which is why it needs checking.
- **A4 is what would settle it.** The FY2026-27 archives reconcile to
  `secondary_sku_line` annually at ₹0. A monthly reconciliation would either
  confirm the correction or expose it, and the data to do it already exists.
- **Section B is more important than Section A.** The load has not happened yet
  and can wait. The management reports are running today, and if their monthly
  and quarterly figures are built on a naive parse of 56,029 transposed rows,
  people have been reading them for months.
- **B6 decides whose problem this is.** A reader misinterpreting correct serials
  is ours. Sheets holding genuinely transposed values is Prayag's, and belongs in
  their query pack.

## Scale, for context

56,029 rows across the two Segment Wise workbooks — **7.8% of 714,697 rows**. Not
catastrophic, not negligible. And because a month can lose some of its own early
days while gaining days from another, two months can be wrong in opposite
directions while the year still balances exactly.

## Section C stays held

Implementation waits for B3 to be evidenced. B1, B2 and B4 through B9 are agreed
and need no further discussion, but nothing should be built until the date
question is closed — the load would otherwise bake the same interpretation into a
new table.
