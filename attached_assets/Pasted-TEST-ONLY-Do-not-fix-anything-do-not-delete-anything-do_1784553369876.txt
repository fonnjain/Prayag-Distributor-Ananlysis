HYPOTHESIS TO TEST

Nothing was deleted from the July tab. The Serial no column was renumbered -
by a sort, a filter, an insert, or a manual renumber - and because line_uid
includes serial_no, every row became a NEW line_uid and the sync re-ingested
the same invoices as fresh rows.

Why I suspect this rather than deletion:

  normal sync days   Jul 15: 371   Jul 16: 211   Jul 17: 204   Jul 19: 182
  spike days         Jul 18: 1,385   Jul 20: 3,803

3,803 in one morning is twenty times the normal rate. Both spikes fall on days
SALE SHEET 26-27 was edited - 18 July 12:19 and 20 July 05:06.

A DELETION cannot produce an insert spike. Removing rows makes the sync insert
FEWER rows, not 3,803 more. Something made the sheet's rows look new.

And 3,129 rows in the sheet against 6,156 in the DB is close to two copies of
the same data under different serial numbers.

QUERY 1 - THE DECISIVE ONE

  SELECT COUNT(*) AS raw_rows,
         COUNT(DISTINCT (invoice_no, item_code, color, taxable_value))
           AS distinct_lines
  FROM sale_line
  WHERE fy = '2026-27' AND month_label = 'Jul-26';

  distinct near 3,129 while raw is 6,156  -> duplicates under reassigned
                                             serials. Nothing was deleted.
  distinct also near 6,156                -> genuinely different lines. The
                                             deletion story holds.
