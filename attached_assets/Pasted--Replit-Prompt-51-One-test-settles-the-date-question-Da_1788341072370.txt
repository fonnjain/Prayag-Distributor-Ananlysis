# Replit Prompt 51 — One test settles the date question

**Date:** 2 September 2026
**Follows:** Prompt 50, which correctly withdrew the circular transpose rule.
**Scope:** Read-only. One test. Change nothing.

**Standing rule:** every figure states which database or source it came from.

## The hypothesis

Not a transpose rule inferred from the fiscal year — that was circular and was
rightly withdrawn. A **mechanism**, which the row split already points at.

Your own Prompt 50 figures:

| | Rows | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Text dates | 558,440 | 78% |
| Numeric serials | 156,255 | 22% |

And **all 56,029** of the disputed rows have both date components ≤ 12, while the
safe text dates are the ones whose first component exceeds 12.

That is the signature of a **locale mis-parse on import into Google Sheets**:

- `04-01-2025` — 4 January in DD-MM — read as US MM/DD becomes **1 April 2025**
  and converts to a serial.
- `25-04-2025` — 25 cannot be a month, so Sheets **cannot parse it** and leaves
  it as text.

The file would then split exactly where it does: everything with a day above 12
survived as text and is correct; everything with a day of 12 or below was
silently converted with day and month swapped.

## Why this is different from what was withdrawn

The withdrawn rule reasoned **from the fiscal year**: reinterpret until the date
falls inside the FY. Circular, and it could not resolve the 100,226 rows where
both readings are already inside.

This reasons **from the import mechanism**: a specific, testable prediction about
what a mis-parse can and cannot produce. It applies uniformly to all 156,255
numeric rows, and T4 then uses the fiscal year only as a **check on the result**,
never as an input to the rule.

If T1 is zero and T4 comes back near zero, that is a rule derived independently
of the fiscal year that nonetheless produces clean fiscal years. That is
corroboration rather than assumption.

## Either answer is useful

**Zero** — the mechanism is proven, the scope widens from 56,029 to 156,255, and
the correction becomes consistent. It also becomes a question for Prayag: their
source data was corrupted on import into Google Sheets, and it may still be
happening.

**Non-zero** — the theory dies cleanly, the serials are literal, and the position
becomes "take the workbook year as authoritative and leave the dates alone". That
is defensible and it is where Prompt 50 landed.

## What it does not resolve either way

`mgmtMonthIndex` is `(d.getUTCMonth() - 3 + 12) % 12` with **no year check**. A
row is bucketed by calendar month regardless of its year. So under the literal
reading, 56,029 rows dated outside their workbook's fiscal year are being counted
inside it anyway.

Taking the workbook year as authoritative — which is effectively what the code
already does — resolves the year. It does not resolve the month, and the month is
what the reports are built on.
