THE 45 PER CENT BLAST RADIUS IS THE TELL.
A supervised pass at blastRadiusLimitPct=45 is exactly what was done on 30 July
at 35 - it cleared 3,749 rows and the corruption returned within 48 hours.

A guard that must be overridden to let the fix through is measuring the wrong
thing. Under a full replace there is no blast radius at all, because replacing a
month is not a partial mutation.

BUILD THIS INSTEAD:

1. NIGHTLY FULL REPLACE OF THE OPEN MONTH.
   Read every row for the current open month, verify the count, then in ONE
   transaction delete that month and insert the read in full.
   No identity key. No dedup. No tombstone. No supersede. No revive.
   If the sheet holds two identical rows, both are written.
   Acceptance is one line: rows written equals rows read, exactly.

2. THE ONLY GUARD: verify before deleting. If the read returns materially fewer
   rows than the last successful read of that month, ABORT and leave the data
   alone. Store that count in the DATABASE, not memory - an in-memory baseline
   lapses on restart, which has already happened once.
   The delete and insert in one transaction means a partial write cannot occur.

3. FREEZE A MONTH ON THE 7th OF THE FOLLOWING MONTH - seven days of grace for
   late entries, then permanent.
     Apr-26 -> frozen 7 May    May-26 -> frozen 7 Jun
     Jun-26 -> frozen 7 Jul    Jul-26 -> frozen 7 Aug
   Derive the date from today, never a config list. A frozen month is skipped
   entirely - no read, no write. Record its row count and total, and assert them
   on startup.
   Under this rule April, May and June should ALREADY be frozen, which would
   have prevented their churn entirely.

4. JULY NEEDS NO SUPERVISED TOMBSTONE. The first nightly replace corrects it:
   delete 19,092 rows, insert 11,454, done. No override, no dry-run review, no
   blast-radius exception.

WHAT SURVIVES FROM #74:

WHAT IS DROPPED:
