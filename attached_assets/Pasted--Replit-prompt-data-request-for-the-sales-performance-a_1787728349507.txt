## P1 — Blocking

### 1.1 Salesperson attribution on the sales line

**This is the single biggest gap.** The primary sales register has **no salesperson column of any kind** — only `head_canon`, which has 12 distinct values. Sales can be attributed to a State Head but **not to any of the 178 active individuals** below them.

The only place an individual name appears anywhere in the export is `06_secondary_order_line.csv`, which has `sales_user_name` and `sales_user_id` — and covers **1–19 August 2026 only**, 8,602 rows. Nineteen days is not a performance record.

Without this, none of the following can be produced: per-salesperson revenue, per-salesperson growth or decline, poor-performer identification, or cost-to-serve per head.

Please provide **one** of the following, in order of preference:

- **(a)** The salesperson (`person_id` / `employee_code`) on each **invoice line or invoice header**, for both fiscal years. Best option by far.
- **(b)** `sales_user_name` / `sales_user_id` on the **full secondary order history**, not just August 2026 — as far back as the CRM holds it, across both the PSCode 3 and Product-Wise eras.
- **(c)** A **historically effective-dated** customer-to-person assignment: `customer_id`, `person_id`, `effective_from`, `effective_to`. The current `13_customer_assignment` has every row stamped `effective_from = 2026-08-15` from `seed_import`, so it is a snapshot with no history — I cannot use it to attribute a sale made in October 2025 to whoever owned the account then.

If none of these exist, say so plainly. It changes what the analysis can honestly claim, and I will scope the salesperson questions out rather than fake them.

### 1.2 Travel and expense data

**There is nothing at all in the current export on travel, expenses, attendance or field visits.** No table, no column. So "travel cost" and "useless travel" cannot be approached even approximately.

Please export whatever exists:

- **Expense claims**: `person_id`, `claim_date`, `expense_type` (travel / fuel / lodging / daily allowance / other), `amount`, `from_location`, `to_location`, `distance_km`, `approval_status`, `approved_amount`
- **Travel or tour plans**: planned itinerary per person per period, if the business runs one
- **Salary or cost-to-company per person**, or at least per grade — needed for cost-to-serve; a grade-level band is fine if individual figures are sensitive

If expenses live in a separate HR or accounting system rather than this app, please say which system and export from there if you can reach it read-only.

### 1.3 Field visit / beat data

To distinguish productive travel from unproductive travel I need visits, not just costs.

Please export, per visit: `person_id`, `visit_date`, `visit_time`, `retailer_id` (RET#) or `customer_id`, `visit_type` (planned / unplanned), `outcome` (order taken / no order / not met), `order_id` where one resulted, and **geotag / latitude / longitude if the CRM captures it**.

Also: the **beat plan** — which retailers each person is supposed to cover, at what frequency.

With visits plus orders I can measure visit-to-order conversion, visits producing no order, and coverage against beat plan. Without visits, "useless travel" is not measurable by any route — spend alone cannot distinguish a wasted trip from a necessary one.

### 1.4 Retailer identity on FY2025-26 secondary

Retailer churn — "which retailers were dropped" — is currently **not measurable across the year boundary**.

`retailer_id` is populated on only **12–15% of FY2025-26** secondary rows but **100% of FY2026-27** rows. The apparent jump from 480 to 6,194 active retailers between Apr–Jul 2025 and Apr–Jul 2026 is entirely a data-capture artefact, not growth. Any churn number computed across that boundary today would be fiction.

Please backfill `retailer_id` (RET#) onto the FY2025-26 secondary rows from the source CRM, or supply the mapping needed to do it. If the FY2025-26 source genuinely never carried RET#, confirm that — I will then measure retailer churn only from August 2026 forward and say so.

## P2 — Materially improves the analysis

### 2.1 Retailer and distributor master with lifecycle dates

To separate "dropped" from "never existed" and from "renamed", I need: `retailer_id` / `customer_id`, name, `status`, `date_onboarded`, `date_closed` or `date_inactive`, reason where recorded, assigned distributor, and assigned person.

Note that retailer name matching is already known to be unreliable — only 273 of 718 FY2025-26 traders matched the CRM master on exact name, and looser matching made it worse. So please key on RET# / customer_id, not name.

### 2.2 Full secondary order history

`06_secondary_order_line.csv` covers 1–19 August 2026 only. The full history would deliver three things at once:

- salesperson attribution (see 1.1b)
- order-to-invoice conversion and service level
- **it would settle the month-end concentration question.** 65–72% of invoice value lands in the last five days of the month. The current order file never reaches a month end, so it cannot test whether that is commercial ordering behaviour or a billing artefact.

### 2.3 Order-to-invoice linkage

If an order line can be tied to the invoice line that fulfilled it — `order_id` → `invoice_no`, with dates — I can measure fill rate, lead time, and orders that were never fulfilled. That is a much better "red flag" source than inferring from stockouts.

### 2.4 Returns, credit notes and rejections

Nothing in the current export covers goods coming back. Please export credit notes and sales returns at line level: date, customer, item, qty, value, reason. A rising return rate on a customer or an item is one of the clearest early red flags available, and right now it is invisible.

### 2.5 Receivables and outstanding ageing

Also entirely absent. Per customer: outstanding balance, ageing buckets (0–30 / 31–60 / 61–90 / 90+), credit limit, days beyond terms. A customer growing in revenue while ageing out on payment is a red flag that revenue analysis alone cannot see.

### 2.6 Scheme payouts actually paid

`22_schemes.csv` holds scheme definitions and slabs, but not what was **paid**. Please export actual scheme settlements: customer, scheme, period, qualifying volume, amount credited, settlement date. Discount analysis showed net price variation is very tight (IQR 98.7–101.8 on a mix-neutral index) — so if real commercial variation exists between customers, it is likely to be in scheme payouts rather than invoice price.
