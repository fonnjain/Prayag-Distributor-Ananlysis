# Replit Prompt 101 — AI Sales Plan

**Date:** 16 September 2026
**Scope:** A is read-only. B is the design. C is the build order.

**Standing rule:** every figure states which database or source it came from.

**A new page: `/ai-sales-plan`.** The fourth AI page. `/ai-targets` proposes
targets, `/ai-schemes` proposes schemes, `/ai-plan` proposes where to go. **This
one proposes what to say when you get there.**

---

## The design constraint that shapes everything

The top 80% of the portfolio is **560 codes**. A typical retailer buys **~14
SKUs**. So the naive gap list is **546 items**.

**Nobody can act on 546 lines.** A 30-minute retailer visit covers eight to
twelve SKUs credibly.

So this page is not a gap report. **It is a ranked shortlist with a reason
attached to each line**, and the ranking is the whole product.

The top 80% composition, for reference:

## Section A — read-only, before design is fixed

```
Read-only. Change nothing.

A1. THE CUSTOMER'S OWN HISTORY. For a named retailer, from
    secondary_sku_line:
      FY2025-26: SKUs bought, quantity and value each, months active
      FY2026-27 to date: the same
      which of their SKUs are in the top 80% portfolio and which are not
      which they bought last year and have NOT bought this year

    That last line is the strongest pitch on the page and nobody has it
    today. Print it for three sample retailers.

A2. PEER SETS. H4 records that canonical geography resolves for 46.27% of
    FY2026-27 active retailers after the distributor bridge.

    So a same-region peer set works for under half. Print, for the same
    three retailers:
      same-state peers, if geography resolves
      same-distributor peers - always available, and arguably a better
        comparison than state
      similar-size peers by annual value quintile

    RECOMMEND which peer basis to lead with. My view: same-distributor
    first, because it always resolves and a distributor's other retailers
    are a genuinely comparable set. State second where available.

A3. ADJACENCY. For each retailer, which sub-categories do they buy and
    which do they not?

    A retailer buying CPVC fittings and no CPVC pipe is a different pitch
    from one buying no CPVC at all. Print the sub-category coverage for
    the three samples - how many of 31 they touch.

A4. STATE VERSUS INDIA DEMAND. For the "new customer" tabs, can a SKU's
    demand be shown per state beside all-India?

    Primary sale_line has state_canon. Secondary has the geography gap.
    Say which source can carry a state-level SKU ranking and how complete
    it is.

A5. LAST-YEAR QUANTITY AS A REFERENCE POINT. Confirm FY2025-26
    retailer-level quantity is available per SKU, since the whole
    "buy more" pitch rests on it.

    Note H2: August 2026 secondary SKU is empty, and FY2026-27 runs only to
    July. So the current-year picture has a gap that must be stated.

A6. HOW MANY RETAILERS have enough history to plan against? A retailer with
    two SKUs and one month has no pattern. Print the distribution of SKUs
    per retailer so a minimum threshold can be set.
```

## Section B — the page

```
Design for comment.

TWO AUDIENCES, five tabs. You suggested three plus two; I think five, with
one addition to each side.

=== EXISTING CUSTOMER ===
Selected by retailer, or by member showing their retailers.

TAB 1 - WHAT THEY BUY
  Not a gap list. The salesperson must know this customer BEFORE the gap
  makes sense.

    every SKU this customer bought, FY2025-26 and FY2026-27 to date
    quantity and value, side by side, with the change
    their total, their rank among the member's retailers, months active

    THREE FLAGS, computed:
      BOUGHT LAST YEAR, NOT THIS YEAR   - the strongest pitch available
      DOWN MORE THAN 30%                - slipping
      UP MORE THAN 30%                  - working, do more

    Then, separately: which top-80% SKUs they ALREADY buy, and at what
    quantity against the peer median. "You buy C62, but half what your
    peers buy" is a better opening than a new product.

TAB 2 - BUY MORE OF THE TOP 80%
  Top-80% SKUs this customer does NOT buy, ranked.

    RANKING, in order of weight:
      1. peer penetration - what share of their peer set buys it
      2. adjacency - do they already buy this sub-category
      3. portfolio rank - position within the 560

    SHOW 10, with the top 3 flagged. Not 546.

    Each line carries its reason: "8 of 11 peers at this distributor buy
    it" or "you buy CPVC fittings but no CPVC pipe".

TAB 3 - SKU PEERS
  SKUs their peers buy that they do not, WITHOUT the top-80% restriction.

    Default peer set: same distributor. Toggle to same state where
    geography resolves, and to national.

    STATE THE PEER BASIS AND ITS SIZE ON THE TAB - "compared against 11
    retailers under this distributor". A peer signal from 3 retailers is
    not the same as from 40.

    Ranked by peer penetration, then by peer median quantity.

TAB 4 - NEW SKU
  Products the peer set does NOT buy either, but which sell elsewhere.

    This is the range-extension tab. Ranked by quantity sold elsewhere,
    largest first, as you specified.

    Add one filter the salesperson needs: EXCLUDE what this region cannot
    sell. A SKU selling only in Kerala is not a pitch in Bihar.

    If geography does not resolve for this retailer, say so and fall back
    to national with that stated.

=== NEW CUSTOMER ===
No purchase history exists, so ranking must come from the market.

TAB 5 - TOP 80%, STATE VS INDIA
  The 560 top-80% SKUs with TWO demand columns side by side:

    what this SKU does in THIS STATE      value, quantity, rank
    what it does ACROSS INDIA             value, quantity, rank

    Sorted by state demand, so a new customer is pitched what actually
    moves where they are.

    THE GAP BETWEEN THE TWO COLUMNS IS THE INSIGHT. A SKU ranked 12th
    nationally and 180th in this state is either an opportunity or a
    product that does not suit the market. Flag large divergences both
    ways.

TAB 6 - THE REST, BY NATIONAL PERFORMANCE
  Everything outside the top 80%, ranked by all-India sales.

    Same two columns. This is the long tail for a customer who wants
    breadth beyond the core range.

EVERY TAB
  - source named on every figure
  - H2 stated where FY2026-27 secondary is incomplete - August empty, data
    to July only
  - peer basis and peer count always visible
  - never a zero where a figure is unavailable
  - export to Excel, one workbook per customer, so it can be carried
```

## Section C — build order

```
C1  Section A. It may change the peer basis and the minimum-history
    threshold.

C2  TAB 1 - what they buy. Ships alone and is immediately useful. A
    salesperson walking in knowing what this customer bought last year and
    has stopped buying is better prepared than anything else on this page.

C3  TAB 5 - top 80%, state vs India. Needs no customer history, so it
    works for every new prospect from day one.

C4  TAB 2 - buy more of the top 80%. Needs the ranking engine.

C5  TAB 3 - SKU peers. Needs peer sets, which is the hard part.

C6  TAB 4 and TAB 6 - the extension tabs.

C2 and C3 are worth shipping even if nothing after them is built.
```

## Notes

- **Tab 1 before Tab 2, deliberately.** You listed the gap first, but a
  salesperson who does not know what the customer already buys cannot use a gap
  list. "You bought 200 of C62 last year and nothing this year" is a stronger
  opening than any recommendation.
- **The peer basis matters more than the algorithm.** Same-distributor peers
  always resolve and are genuinely comparable — same market, same supply route.
  Same-state peers only resolve for 46.27% of retailers under H4. I would lead
  with distributor.
- **Tab 5's two columns are the point of that tab.** A SKU ranked 12th nationally
  and 180th in a state is a question worth asking. Showing only one column answers
  nothing.
- **Ten per tab, three flagged.** 546 lines is a report nobody reads. Ten lines
  with a reason each is a visit plan.
