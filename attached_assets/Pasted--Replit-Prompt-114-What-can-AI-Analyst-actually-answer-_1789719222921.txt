# Replit Prompt 114 — What can AI Analyst actually answer?

**Date:** 18 September 2026
**Scope:** Read-only throughout. Change nothing.

**Standing rule:** every figure states which database or source it came from.

**Why.** AI Analyst was built months ago. Since then the app has gained three
fiscal years of secondary order booking, the six-master category registry, the
Resolution register, GP margin, effective-dated MRP, the peer-set computations,
the seasonal calibration, and the pending-order attribution hierarchy.

**It is reportedly not working well.** Before adding anything, establish what it
can reach today and what it cannot.

---

## Section A — what it is

```
Read-only.

A1. WHAT IS AI ANALYST? Print its implementation - the route, the builder,
    the model call, and what a request and response look like end to end.

    Is it a fixed-question interface, free text, or a payload-driven
    narrative generator?

A2. WHAT DATA CAN IT REACH? Print every table, view or computed measure it
    can query, and everything it cannot.

    The critical question: does it query the database, or is it handed a
    prepared payload? Those are very different capabilities.

A3. IF IT IS PAYLOAD-DRIVEN, print the payload schema. Field by field,
    what it contains and from which source.

A4. WHAT QUESTIONS CAN IT ANSWER TODAY? Give ten real examples that work,
    with their actual answers.

A5. WHAT HAPPENS WHEN IT CANNOT ANSWER? Does it say so, guess, or produce
    a plausible but unsupported answer?

    The AI Reports surface was found leaking raw payload paths -
    [performance.totalOB], [identity.workingDays] - with "5 of 205 tokens
    could not be matched". Does AI Analyst have the same failure mode?

A6. WHEN WAS IT LAST CHANGED? If its payload was fixed before the category
    registry, the Resolution register or GP margin existed, it cannot see
    them.
```

## Section B — what it is missing

```
Read-only. For each, state: REACHABLE TODAY, REACHABLE WITH WORK, or NOT
REACHABLE.

Everything below exists in the app now and did not when AI Analyst was
built.

B1. SECONDARY ORDER BOOKING - three fiscal years, 866,201 rows,
    Rs 548 Cr. Retailer and distributor identity on the Product-Wise rows.

B2. THE SIX-MASTER CATEGORY REGISTRY - 10,417 assignments, six masters, 31
    sub-categories, with Unmapped explicit.

B3. THE RESOLUTION REGISTER - holds and pending items, with blocks_api.
    Can AI Analyst see that PTMT January-April margin is held? If not, it
    could narrate a figure the app itself refuses to show.

B4. GP MARGIN - margin_fact, FY2025-26 complete, FY2026-27 to June, with
    the PTMT hold.

B5. EFFECTIVE-DATED MRP - mrp_history for closed periods, mrp_synced for
    the open FY, and the not-offered exclusion rule.

B6. THE SEASONAL CALIBRATION - Q4 is 34.56% of the year. Can it project
    correctly, or would it annualise linearly?

B7. PEER SETS AND CUSTOMER-SKU PENETRATION - the Prompt 105 shared
    computations.

B8. THE PENDING-ORDER ATTRIBUTION HIERARCHY - head, member, unassigned,
    disputed, with reconciliation.

B9. THE TOP-80 SNAPSHOT - 564 codes, Rs 112.00 Cr, frozen and dated.

B10. THE PARITY AND FRESHNESS SIGNALS - MRP coverage, unpriced-code counts,
     environment parity.
```

## Section C — the questions it should be able to answer

```
Read-only. For each, say whether AI Analyst can answer it today, and if
not, what is missing.

These are questions a management team would actually ask.

PERFORMANCE
  C1  Which state heads are ahead of plan and which are behind, and by how
      much?
  C2  Is the business growing? Show the monthly shape, not just the YTD
      figure.
  C3  Which members' order booking is not converting into dispatch?
  C4  What is our projected year-end, seasonally adjusted?

PRODUCT
  C5  Which categories are growing and which are shrinking?
  C6  Which products are we selling that we cannot price?
  C7  Where is our margin concentrated, and where is it thin?
  C8  Which SKUs are sold by only one or two retailers?

CUSTOMER
  C9  Which retailers stopped buying this year?
  C10 Which distributors have pending orders with no assigned member?
  C11 Which customers buy narrowly compared with their peers?

RISK
  C12 What figures are currently unreliable, and why?
  C13 Where is our revenue concentrated?
  C14 What data are we missing, and what is it worth?

C12 and C14 are the interesting ones. The Resolution register holds
exactly that, and if AI Analyst cannot read it, the app knows something
its analyst does not.
```

## Section D — the shape of a fix

```
Recommend, do not build.

D1. Is AI Analyst's problem THE PAYLOAD - it cannot see enough - or THE
    PROMPT, it can see enough and does not use it well?

    Those need different fixes and the distinction should come from
    evidence, not assumption.

D2. Should it query, or be handed a payload? A payload is safe and
    bounded. Query access is far more capable and far more dangerous -
    it could read held data, or compute a figure the app deliberately
    suppresses.

    Recommend, with the Resolution register in mind.

D3. WHAT WOULD IT COST to give it the ten capabilities in Section B? Rank
    them by value against effort.

D4. WHAT MUST IT NEVER DO?
      narrate a held figure - H1 PTMT margin, any blocks_api item
      annualise linearly
      present order booking as sales
      state a percentage without its basis
      infer a category for an unmapped code
      treat an unavailable figure as zero

    Those are the operating rules. Can they be enforced in the payload, or
    only in the prompt? Payload enforcement is stronger.

Report only.
```

---

## Notes

- **A2 is the question everything turns on.** A payload-driven analyst can only
  answer what someone anticipated. A querying analyst can answer what it is asked.
  The failure mode described — "not working well" — usually means the first.
- **B3 matters more than it sounds.** If AI Analyst cannot see the Resolution
  register, it can confidently narrate PTMT margin for January to April, a figure
  the app itself refuses to display and which we know is overstated by roughly 21
  points.
- **C12 and C14 are the best test.** "What is currently unreliable and why" is
  answerable from the register alone, and it is exactly what a management team
  should be able to ask.
- **D4 is the durable part.** Six prohibitions, all from the operating rules. If
  they can be enforced in the payload rather than the prompt, they survive a model
  change.
