The data-availability table and the FY2023-24 finding are both accepted -
137,619 rows with month_label only, zero invoice_date, zero invoice_no, from a
10-column source schema. That is a permanent limit on daily analysis for that
year, not a fixable gap.

But your segment table shows something your summary understates, and it should
be recorded.

THE FESTIVAL-WEEK DIP IS CONSISTENT. THE BUILD IS NOT.

Week 0, both dated years:
                   FY2024-25   FY2025-26
    PTMT             0.813       0.279
    CP               0.547       0.263
    Sanitaryware     0.690       0.052
    CPVC             0.942       0.120
    SWR              1.373       0.169

Four of five segments below normal in both years. Sanitaryware at 0.052 is a
95% collapse.

And FY2024-25 week 0 runs 29 Oct - 4 Nov, which CONTAINS the October month-end.
PTMT, CP and Sanitaryware still fall below normal in a week holding the biggest
selling days of the month. That is a stronger signal than the raw multiple
suggests.

The company figure of 1.199 hides this because SWR pulls it up at 1.373 - and
SWR is drainage pipe, a construction material, not a household festival
purchase. Its behaving differently supports the mechanism.

RESTATE THE CONCLUSION AS TWO SEPARATE FINDINGS:
  1. Pre-festival BUILD - not established. One year, contaminated by month-end.
  2. Festival-week DIP - consistent across both dated years and every household
     segment, and present in FY2024-25 despite month-end contamination.

WHAT TO DO WITH IT
Do not build a festival curve on this. But when the festival week arrives -
5-11 November 2026, around Diwali on the 8th - a very weak week in PTMT, CP and
Sanitaryware is EXPECTED, not a performance problem.

That is worth a note on the Momentum page rather than a code change: one line
saying the week containing a major festival is historically 30-70% below normal
in household segments. It stops someone reading a normal festival week as a
collapse.
