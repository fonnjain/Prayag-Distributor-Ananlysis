# Replit Prompt 34 — Are the split-state suffixes head initials?

**Date:** 1 September 2026
**Scope:** Read-only. Change nothing, register nothing, remap nothing.

**Standing rule:** every figure states which database or source it came from.

## The hypothesis

`stateHeadPackCoverageCheck.ts` states:

> *"Current-pack spellings that are geographic sub-territories, not distinct
> states."*

That comment may be wrong. The Prompt 31 A5 evidence points elsewhere:

| Order-book label | Head the data attributes it to |
|---|---|
| `UP ( A )` | **A**nant Singh — 14/14 customers, plus coverage and assignments |
| `UP (AS)` | **A**nuj **S**harma — 8 customers |
| `UP ( R )` | UTTAR PRADESH attributes to Syed Aqil **R**izvi, 60 customers |

Three labels, three matching initials. And it extends: `RAJASTHAN (N)` →
**N**arendra Kumar Sharma, who holds open Rajasthan coverage. `KARNATAKA (B)` →
**B**iju C.O, whom Prashant Onam Naik reports to. `MAHARASTRA L` → **L**alan, who
holds the 10 Maharashtra distributors. `MAHARASTRA R` → **R**izvi, who holds 149
Maharashtra dealers and owns Maharashtra 2 in the dashboard.

If this holds, these suffixes encode **who covers the territory**, not where it
is — and collapsing them to a plain state name destroys head attribution, not
just geographic detail.

Some are clearly still geographic: `DELHI NCR`, `JAMMU`, `KASHMIR`, `AP`. So the
vocabulary is mixed, which is worse than either alternative.

## Notes

- A2 is the test. Three labels already fit; sixteen tested at once either
  confirms the pattern or breaks it, and a broken pattern tells us as much.
- C1 is the question that decides severity. If `head_canon` carries the head on
  those same Maharashtra rows, then collapsing `MAHARASTRA L` to `MAHARASHTRA`
  lost nothing — the head survived in another column. If it does not, ₹20.81
  crore lost its head attribution at ingest.
- C4 changes the UP fix again. If A means Anant, then `territoryResolver`'s
  `"UP (A)" → ["WUP"]` is not a spacing bug with a correct target — it is the
  wrong person, and correcting the spacing would have routed Anant's customers to
  Lalan. That was the right call to withdraw it.
- D2 is deliberately pointed. The instinct will be to register L, R and S as
  hierarchy children. If they are people, that repeats the original mistake in a
  more permanent place.

## For the Prayag pack, once this comes back

Query C4 currently asks how many Maharashtra territories exist. If the hypothesis
holds, the better question is narrower and easier for them to answer:

> Do the letters in MAHARASTRA L / R / S refer to the state heads covering each
> part — L for Lalan, R for Rizvi? If so, who is S, and is Maharashtra 2 the same
> as R?

That is a question a sales manager can answer in one line, unlike "how many
territories are there".
