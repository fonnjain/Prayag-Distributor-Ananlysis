## Section B — the ledger. First build step.

D8 puts this first and it is right: a durable per-run record has to exist before
any prior-month replacement is automated, or a shrink becomes unauditable after
the fact.

```
Code change. One thing: a durable ingest ledger for the primary monthly
register. Show the diff before applying.

The secondary dashboard already has this - a durable ingest-run record with
append-only row revisions. The primary register does not; it persists only
the latest baseline and frozen anchor, with rows-read and rows-written held
in runtime results.

Build the equivalent for the primary path. Per run, per affected month,
record durably:

  - run identity, scheduler or operator, timestamp
  - month label and fiscal year
  - rows read from source, rows written
  - before and after row counts and values
  - rows ADDED, REMOVED and CHANGED as counts
  - before and after fingerprints
  - source spreadsheet id, tab, and a content hash
  - outcome: replaced, frozen-skipped, frozen-anchored, aborted-short-read,
    failed

MAKE EVERY SHRINK VISIBLE. Any nonzero reduction in rows or value must be
recorded and surfaced, regardless of size. The 98% guard stays as a
catastrophic-read guard only - it must not be treated as approval for
normal shrinkage. A 0.2% deletion currently passes silently and that is the
gap this closes.

DO NOT:
  - change the sync scope, the freeze clock, or openMonthLabels
  - change SHORT_READ_TOLERANCE
  - change the replacement mechanism itself
  - unfreeze anything

This is instrumentation. Behaviour stays identical.

AFTER APPLYING, print the ledger schema, a sample record from a dry run, and
confirm no sync behaviour changed.

Then publish and push.
```

## Section C — the remaining sequence. Do not start until B is live.

One step at a time, each with its own diff and publish. Listed in order.

```
For reference, not to be built yet. Confirm the order and flag anything that
should move.

C1. INGEST-GENERATION CACHE KEYS
    AI cache keys lack a data-version token, so a report can stay stale after
    a prior-month correction. Add an ingest generation to report and AI cache
    keys, and to dashboard snapshots.

C2. TARGETED INVALIDATION
    There is no dependency-aware invalidation for reports affected by a
    prior-month correction. Add invalidation scoped to the affected periods.

C3. PERSISTED WEEKLY SCHEDULER
    Polling interval plus a persisted weekly run key, following the digest
    pattern. A restart may initialise the scheduler but must NOT decide that
    work is due - the MRP refresh fired 18 times in 10 days on deployments
    and that must not repeat. Reuse an overlap guard or shared lock against
    the existing six-hour jobs.

C4. PROSPECTIVE FREEZE CLOCK
    Apply from Sep-26 FORWARD only. Better still, make persisted frozen or
    anchored state override the calculated clock, so a month can never
    reopen regardless of how the clock is later changed. That is
    structurally safer than getting a date right.

    Freeze on the 1st, not the 15th, so scope-exit and immutability coincide.

C5. AUTOMATIC PRIOR-MONTH REPLACEMENT
    Only after C1-C4. Current month plus one immediately prior, automatic,
    with every shrink recorded and alerted. Months two and three prior stay
    on the controlled frozen-drift approval path.

Confirm this order, and say if anything must move earlier.
```

## Notes

- **Section A is the only urgent item.** August is frozen, the source has moved by
  492 rows, and the controlled path exists. That is a decision this week, not a
  build.
- **Section B before anything else in the sequence.** Once prior-month replacement
  is automated, a silent deletion becomes unauditable. The ledger makes it
  visible first.
- **C4's second form is better than the first.** Applying the clock prospectively
  depends on choosing the right cut-over date; making persisted frozen state
  override the calculated clock makes reopening structurally impossible. I would
  take that.
- The 15th is dropped. Freezing on the 1st of the fourth month aligns
  immutability with scope-exit, which the report is right that the 15th does not.

## What this does not solve, and should be said

The recommendation gives **current month plus one prior automatically**. Prayag
edit for about two months. So some drift will still occur — it will simply be
recorded and visible rather than silent, and months two and three prior will be
reconciled by explicit decision instead of by schedule.

That is the right trade. The alternative keeps four months mutable and delays
every anchor by roughly 92 days.
