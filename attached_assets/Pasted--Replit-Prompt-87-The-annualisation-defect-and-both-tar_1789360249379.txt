## Section A — the page says the business is shrinking. It is not.

The Engine Generated context line reads:

> *"FY2024-25: −2.3% nominal / +0.9% real · FY2025-26: +5.8% nominal / +6.1%
> real · **FY2026-27: −10.2% nominal / −15.3% real**"*

**Two of three check out exactly.** FY2024-25 is ₹341.14 Cr against ₹349.02 Cr =
−2.26%. FY2025-26 is ₹360.99 Cr against ₹341.14 Cr = +5.82%.

**The third is computed by linear annualisation**, and it matches:

| Method | Full year | vs FY2025-26 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Linear, YTD × 12/5 | ₹324.84 Cr | **−10.0%** |
| **Seasonal curve, YTD ÷ 34.95%** | **₹387.27 Cr** | **+7.3%** |

The business does **34.56% of its year in Q4** — and the same page states that,
in the Seasonal Calibration block directly above.

So the engine annualises linearly on a business it has already measured as
heavily back-loaded. **The sign is wrong and the figure is out by roughly 17
percentage points**, and it is the context a CEO reads while setting targets.

```
Read-only first, then fix.

A1. Print the exact calculation behind the FY2026-27 context figure.
    Confirm it is YTD x 12 / elapsed months.

A2. Confirm the other two years are computed from complete actuals, not
    annualised. They reconcile exactly, so they should be.

A3. THE FIX. Annualise using the seasonal curve the page already holds -
    Apr-Aug is 34.95% of a year - not linearly.

    Print the corrected figure. Expect approximately Rs 387 Cr and +7.3%
    nominal.

A4. STATE THAT IT IS A PROJECTION. A completed year and a projected year
    must not sit in the same list looking alike. Label it "projected" and
    name the basis.

A5. The real-terms conversion is arithmetically correct - (1-0.102)/1.059
    gives -15.2%. It will follow the corrected nominal automatically.
    Confirm.

A6. Where else does this annualisation appear? Any run-rate, projection or
    full-year estimate anywhere in the app computed as YTD x 12 / months
    is wrong on this business. Print every occurrence.

A6 matters most. If linear annualisation is used elsewhere, every one of
those figures understates by a similar margin.
```

## Notes

- **A6 is the question with the widest reach.** If linear annualisation is used for
  run-rates or projections anywhere else, every one of those figures understates
  by a similar margin on this business.
- **A4 matters independently of the arithmetic.** Two completed years and one
  projected year in the same list, formatted identically, invite a comparison that
  is not valid even once the projection is right.
- **B10 is the question the restructure depends on.** One engine divides a total,
  the other builds one up. Tabs are fine; presenting them as the same kind of
  number is not.
- **B5 is worth pinning before anything moves.** If target edits propagate into
  alert denominators, moving the engine that proposes them changes what fires.
