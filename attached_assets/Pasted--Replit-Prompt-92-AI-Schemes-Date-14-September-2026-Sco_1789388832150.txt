## The finding this is built on

**A scarce SKU is not one customers buy little of. It is one almost nobody
buys.**

| Band | Codes | Sales per code | Customers per SKU | Value per pair |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| VERY HIGH | 122 | ₹55.1 L | 158.8 | ₹34,671 |
| HIGH | 437 | ₹9.23 L | 43.7 | ₹21,113 |
| MEDIUM | 958 | ₹2.10 L | 11.1 | ₹18,958 |
| LOW | 1,041 | ₹51,681 | 3.2 | ₹16,150 |
| SCARCE | 1,423 | ₹9,487 | 1.3 | ₹7,298 |
| DORMANT | 6,464 | — | 0 | — |

Sales per code differ **580×** top to bottom. Customers per SKU differ **122×**.
Value per pair differs only **4.8×**.

**Distribution explains the gap, not order size.** So a percentage discount aimed
at making someone buy *more* of a scarce SKU targets the wrong variable.

**Breadth is the lever, and secondary is where it is visible:**

| | Primary | Secondary |
|---|---:|---:|
| Customers | 572 distributors | **9,078 retailers** |
| SKUs each | 95 | **~14** |
| Pair density | 2.4% | **0.35%** |

**+2 SKUs per retailer ≈ ₹8.29 Cr** of secondary order booking, valued at a
**marginal** pair of ₹4,568 — not the ₹18,271 average, which is dragged up by a
small number of large pairs.

```
    retailers per SKU, by band.

A4. PEER SETS. Can a comparable-retailer set be built? What attributes
    exist - state, distributor, size band, segments bought, months active?

    With 9,078 retailers a peer set should be statistically real. With 572
    distributors it would not be.

A5. MARGIN JOIN. Scheme depth is capped by margin, which lives in
    margin_fact keyed on item_code. secondary_sku_line is keyed on item
    code too.

    Confirm the two join. Print coverage: how many sold SKUs have a
    margin figure, and what share of value they carry.

    KNOWN GAPS: margin stops at June 2026, PTMT Jan-Apr is held H1, and
    780 codes worth Rs 9.36 Cr have no MRP at all (P3).

A6. CANNIBALISATION. Is there any way to distinguish a retailer adding a
    SKU from a retailer switching between two similar SKUs?

    If not, say so. An engine that cannot tell incremental from
    substitution will confidently recommend paying for both.

A7. What does the existing nudge engine already compute that this can
    reuse? territoryResolver, audienceFilter, the ROI filter, the slab
    arithmetic.
```

## Section B — the model

```
Design, for comment. Build nothing.

TWO INDEPENDENT AXES. Depth is set by margin; posture is set by band.
They must not be collapsed - every band contains every margin tier.

  VERY HIGH   26.55 Cr rich | 26.59 mid | 14.03 thin | 0 bare
  SCARCE       0.49 Cr rich |  0.58 mid |  0.26 thin | 0.02 bare

AXIS 1 - MARGIN TIER sets the CEILING. Cap scheme cost at ~12-15% of the
category's gross margin:

  RICH  >=55%   PTMT 68.5, Connection 59.3, Water tank 56.9   max 8%
  MID   40-55%  CPVC ft 49.5, Cistern 48.7, AGRI 42.0         max 6%
  THIN  27-40%  CP 40.0, UPVC 39.7, SWR 34.3, Sink 30.6       max 4%
  BARE  <27%    Garden pipe 14.3, CPVC pipe 18.6, AGRI 18.3   NO CASH
                                                              SCHEME

  Note the pipe/fitting split: CPVC fittings 49.5% vs CPVC pipe 18.6%.
  AGRI fittings 42.0% vs AGRI pipe 18.3%. Same family, different economics.

AXIS 2 - BAND sets the POSTURE:

  VERY HIGH   NO push scheme. 122 codes at 159 retailers each - there is
              no breadth left to buy. A 3% scheme costs Rs 2.02 Cr to
              subsidise sales that happen anyway.
  HIGH        Protect only. Light threshold slabs to hold share.
  MEDIUM      The main prize. 958 codes at ~11 retailers each. Breadth
              push within the margin cap.
  LOW         Aggressive breadth within cap.
  SCARCE      NO CASH. Non-price levers only - see below.
  DORMANT     Trial and sampling. Not a percentage scheme.

THREE MECHANICS, and two of them cost nothing:

  1. FIRST-ORDER RELIEF
     A one-time incentive on a retailer's FIRST purchase of a target SKU.
     Paid once per pair, never again. The pair, once created, is worth
     Rs 4,568 in its first year and more thereafter.

  2. QUALIFYING BREADTH CONDITION - free
     The main volume slab unlocks only if the order includes n SKUs from
     the target group. A retailer who would have qualified anyway pays you
     nothing and takes the range.

  3. WEIGHTED COUNTING - free
     Target-SKU billing counts 1.5x toward the existing slab. No new cash,
     faster slab crossing. The existing engine already computes that
     arithmetic.

  Mechanics 2 and 3 matter most for BARE-margin categories, which cannot
  fund a cash scheme at any depth.

  scheme_item_group already exists with 22 values, so group scoping is in
  the data model.

TARGETING - the peer gap

  For each (retailer, SKU) pair that does NOT exist:
    does the retailer's peer set buy it?
    peer set = same state + similar size + overlapping segments
    score = peer penetration x SKU margin x band priority

  Rank offers by expected incremental gross contribution, not by SKU size.

PAYBACK - every proposal must pass

  At 45% blended margin:
    2% scheme needs  4.4% incremental volume
    3% scheme needs  6.7%
    5% scheme needs 11.1%
    8% scheme needs 17.8%

  A proposal that cannot state its breakeven is not shown.
```

## Section C — guardrails

```
Non-negotiable. Any of these failing means the proposal is suppressed.

C1. NEVER propose on held data. H1 PTMT Jan-Apr has no usable cost basis -
    margin overstated ~21 points. The Resolution register must be consulted
    at proposal time, not at display time.

C2. NEVER propose where margin is unknown. 780 codes, Rs 9.36 Cr (P3). No
    margin, no cap, no proposal.

C3. NEVER exceed the margin cap. Print the cost as a percentage of gross
    margin on every proposal, not just the scheme rate.

C4. NEVER propose without a breakeven. Cost, required lift, and the
    assumption behind it, on the face of the proposal.

C5. A PROPOSAL IS NOT A SCHEME. Everything lands in a proposals table.
    Nothing becomes live without a person accepting it. Same rule as
    engine_targets.

C6. SCHEME ELIGIBILITY VOCABULARY IS RING-FENCED. scheme_item_group is 22
    separate values, deliberately not the category registry. A category
    remap must never silently change who qualifies. This engine READS it
    and never writes it.

C7. CANNIBALISATION. Until A6 is answered, every proposal carries a
    substitution warning naming the nearest SKUs in the same group.

C8. Do not touch the existing nudge engine. This proposes new schemes; that
    one nudges toward existing slabs. Different jobs.
```

## Section D — the page

```
/ai-schemes. Proposals, not schemes.

  TAB 1  BREADTH OPPORTUNITY
    the pair matrix - density, SKUs per retailer, retailers per SKU
    by band and by category
    the headline: what +1, +2, +3 SKUs per retailer is worth at the
    MEASURED marginal pair value

  TAB 2  PROPOSALS
    ranked by expected incremental gross contribution
    each showing: target SKUs, audience, mechanic, depth, cost, cost as
    % of margin, breakeven lift, peer evidence, substitution warning
    accept / reject / hold, with the decision recorded

  TAB 3  SKU BANDS
    the five bands plus dormant, with codes, value, retailers per SKU,
    margin tier and what each band is eligible for

  TAB 4  MARGIN HEADROOM
    by category: gross margin, max scheme, what is currently being spent
    coverage stated - which months, which categories are held

  TAB 5  ACCEPTED
    proposals that became schemes, and what happened to them afterwards.
    Without this the engine never learns whether it was right.

EVERY FIGURE: source named, coverage stated, three states, held periods
explicit.
```

```
E1  Section A only. Read-only. It may invalidate the design.
E2  The pair matrix and band computation - Tab 1 and Tab 3. No proposals.
    This alone is useful: nobody can currently see that a scarce SKU is
    scarce because 1.3 retailers buy it.

E5  Proposals with the three mechanics - Tab 2.
```

## Notes

- **A3 is the load-bearing check.** I estimated customers-per-SKU by scaling to
  reconcile a known pair total. If the real spread is not roughly 122×, the
  premise weakens and the design should change.
- **Mechanics 2 and 3 are the important ones.** Garden Pipe at 14.3% margin cannot
  fund a cash scheme at any depth, and it is not alone — CPVC pipe is 18.6%, AGRI
  pipe 18.3%. A free mechanic is the only mechanic available there.
- **C1 is why the Resolution register exists.** An engine proposing schemes off
  held margin data would be the most expensive possible consumer of a figure we
  know is wrong.
- **E2 alone justifies the work.** Today nobody can see that the difference between
  a top SKU and a scarce one is 122× in distribution and 4.8× in order size. That
  changes what you do about it.
