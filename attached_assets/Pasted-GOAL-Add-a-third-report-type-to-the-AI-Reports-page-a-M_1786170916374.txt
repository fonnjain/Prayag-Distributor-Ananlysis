GOAL
Add a third report type to the AI Reports page: a MASTER GROWTH REPORT.
Scope selector: whole company, one State Head, or one state.

This report answers one question - where is the next rupee of growth, and
whose name is on it. It is a ranked ledger of quantified opportunities,
not a strategy document. Every line must name entities and carry a figure
computed from the register.

THE HARD CONSTRAINT
The model writes prose ONLY. It never produces a number, a name, a rank or
a total. Every figure is computed in the query layer and passed in. If a
section has no data, render the section with its reason line - never let
the model fill the gap with plausible-sounding text.

Any recommendation that cannot name at least one distributor, retailer or
item code is not a recommendation. Drop it.

SECTIONS, IN THIS ORDER

1  EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
   - Total addressable opportunity for the scope, stated as a RANGE not a
     point estimate, with the conversion assumptions printed beside it
   - The five levers ranked by size, each with its named-entity count
   - The single largest opportunity, named
   - The single largest RISK to existing revenue, named
   - The non-additivity note (see DOUBLE COUNTING below) - always printed,
     never suppressed

2  THE OPPORTUNITY LEDGER
   One table, ranked by value descending. Columns:
     Rank | Lever | Named entity | What to do | Value | Effort | Confidence
   Effort: Low / Medium / High. Confidence: High where the figure is pure
   arithmetic (schemes), Medium where it rests on a stated conversion
   assumption, Low where it rests on peer inference.
   Cap at 40 rows. Print the count of rows omitted and their combined value.

3  ACTIVATE
   - Unassigned retailers by head and district, with counts and the split
     between "distributor already operates here" (fixable this week) and
     "no distributor in district" (coverage item)
   - Distributors below 40% activation, named, with their dormant counts
   - Sizing per distributor: dormant retailers x MEDIAN quarterly order of
     that distributor's ACTIVE retailers x conversion assumption
   - Never use the mean. One large retailer distorts it badly.

4  WIDEN
   - Distributors ranked by range gap VALUE, named, top 20
   - Per distributor: codes bought, peer median, tier 1 and 2 counts, and
     the peer-median quarterly net of those tier 1 and 2 items only
   - Tiers 3 and 4 are EXCLUDED from sizing. They are real opportunities
     but too speculative to put in a total.
   - Segment rollup: which segments carry the most range gap across the
     scope

5  RECOVER
   - At Risk retailers by head, with prior-year value
   - Lost item codes by segment, with prior-year value
   - Sizing: prior-year value x recovery assumption
   - Split At Risk by silence duration: under 3 months, 3 to 6, over 6.
     Recovery odds fall sharply with time and the report should show that
     rather than treat all At Risk names as equivalent.

6  PROTECT  (this is value at risk, NOT upside - never add it to the total)
   - Hidden shrinkers: accounts where value is up and quantity is down,
     named, with both percentages
   - Narrowers: accounts whose item-code count fell most, named, with the
     code count both years
   - Concentration: any distributor above 60% of a state, named
   - Silent distributors: no order in 6+ weeks, ranked by size

7  CLOSE
   - Live scheme nudges in scope, ranked by Extra Earn, with Extra ROI
     beside each and the gap
   - Total Extra Earn available at ROI >= 8%, and separately at 5-8%
   - Exclude blocked accounts from the total and list them separately as
     a collections opportunity with the scheme value as the argument
   - Days remaining to the 25th cut-off, prominently

8  WHERE NOT TO LOOK
   - Any apparent gap that is majority project business, named and sized
   - Any gap concentrated in fewer than 6 customers, named
   This section is mandatory and must never be empty-suppressed. If there
   is nothing to report, print "No project-contaminated or concentrated
   gaps identified in this scope."

9  CAPACITY CHECK
   - Visits the ledger implies, against the visits the team currently makes
   - If the ledger exceeds capacity, say so explicitly and print how far
   - A plan that does not fit the working days is a fiction and the report
     must say that rather than imply everything is achievable

10 ASSUMPTIONS AND LIMITS
   - Every conversion assumption used, with its default and current value
   - What cannot be computed and why (margin, credit, stock)
   - The comparison basis used, and any known basis break

DOUBLE COUNTING - precedence rules, enforced in code
A retailer or distributor may appear in more than one lever's narrative
but must be counted in only ONE lever's total. Precedence, highest first:

   1. CLOSE      (arithmetic, no assumption)
   2. RECOVER    (specific, proven prior purchase)
   3. ACTIVATE   (dormant but never a prior buyer)
   4. WIDEN      (peer inference)

So an At Risk retailer counts in RECOVER, not ACTIVATE. A distributor with
both a scheme position and a range gap counts the scheme in CLOSE and the
range separately in WIDEN, because those are different rupees - but the
same retailer's dormancy is counted once.

Print the deduplication effect in the executive summary: "Levers sum to
Rs X before deduplication; Rs Y after."

SIZING RULES
- Conversion assumptions are USER-ADJUSTABLE with these defaults:
    dormant retailer revival     25%
    At Risk recovery             35%
    range recommendation uptake  40%
  Every one is printed on the report beside the figure it drives.
- Totals are shown as a RANGE: half the assumption to the full assumption.
  A single point estimate will be quoted as a forecast and it is not one.
- NO extrapolation from averages anywhere. Historical comparisons use the
  same fiscal months in prior years, actuals only.
- Project and non-territory business is excluded from all opportunity
  figures. State this in the header of every sized section.

KNOWN BASIS BREAK - handle explicitly
FY2024-25 and FY2025-26 are not on the same channel basis: one customer
(Rs 35.73 Cr) is project-classified in FY2025-26 and was unattributed in
FY2024-25. Any section of this report that compares those two years must
carry the disclosure line already added to the SKU Trends mix table, and
must exclude that customer from both years when computing a YoY figure.
If the section cannot be computed on a like-for-like basis, print the
reason rather than the number.

OUTPUT
- Render on screen, then PDF. PPTX on request (14-18 slides: summary,
  ledger, one slide per lever, where-not-to-look, capacity, assumptions).
- More than one file goes out as a single ZIP.
- Filename: Scope_Period_MasterGrowthReport

VERIFICATION - report all six before saying this is done
1. REPRODUCIBILITY. Generate the same report twice, same scope and period.
   Every number must be identical. If any figure moves, the model is
   generating numbers - stop and fix that before anything else.
2. DEDUPLICATION. Print the pre-dedup and post-dedup totals and the count
   of entities that appeared in more than one lever. Then print 5 of those
   entities showing which lever claimed each and why.
3. NO ORPHAN CLAIMS. Print the count of ledger rows with no named entity
   attached. Must be 0.
4. PROJECT EXCLUSION. Print the total opportunity with and without the
   project-channel filter. The difference must be nameable - print the top
   5 project customers it removes.
5. CAPACITY. For a scope where the ledger exceeds team capacity, paste the
   section showing it. Confirm it states the shortfall rather than
   implying the plan is achievable.
6. BASIS BREAK. Confirm any FY2024-25 vs FY2025-26 comparison in the
   report either carries the disclosure line or prints a reason instead of
   a figure. Paste the rendered text.
