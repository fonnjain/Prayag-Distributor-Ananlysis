## The discrepancy

```
Control                  Rs 1,35,34,00,568.14
Production  neondb       Rs 1,35,23,20,560.20     −Rs 10,80,007.94
Development heliumdb     Rs 1,35,33,65,223.14     −Rs 35,345.00
```

FY2026-27, April to August.

Two things stand out. **Production is thirty times further out than development**, against the same control. And the Company Reports page displays **₹135.23 Cr**, which matches production exactly — so the application and the control have disagreed for some time, and the API only made it visible.

FY2025-26 reconciles to the paisa: 145,613 rows, ₹3,60,99,53,808.51. So the method is sound; something specific to the open year is not.

---

## 1. Identify the rows, not the amount

A difference of ₹10,80,007.94 is a set of invoice lines. Find them.

Report:

- **Row counts** — control versus production versus development, for FY2026-27 Apr–Aug, overall and by month.
- **The difference by month.** If it sits in one month, that narrows it immediately.
- **The specific lines.** Present in one source and not the other, or present in both with different amounts. List them with invoice number, date, code, customer, amount.
- **Whether the difference is one-directional** — production missing rows the control has, production holding rows it should not, or both.

Do not stop at a total. The question is which lines, and a reconciliation that only confirms the gap has not advanced anything.

---

## 2. Why do development and production differ by ₹10.4 lakh?

They were loaded from the same registers. Development lags by weeks and suppresses schedulers, so it should hold **fewer** rows than production — yet it is **closer** to the control.

That is the wrong way round, and it is the most informative fact in this report.

- Are there rows in development that never reached production?
- Are there rows in production that development does not have, which should not be there?
- Did the two diverge at a particular date or load?

---

## 3. Where does the control figure come from?

Report its provenance: which register, which query, when it was frozen, and by whom.

FY2026-27 is an **open year** — months stay writable for four months. So:

- Was the control taken at a moment when the data legitimately differed?
- Has any Apr–Aug month been written to since the control was captured?
- If so, the control is a snapshot of a moving figure, and **the control may be what is stale** rather than production.

State plainly which of the two you believe is correct, and on what evidence.

---

## 4. Rule out the known traps first

Three known issues could produce a difference of this shape. Check each and say which, if any, contributes:

**Water tanks.** 133 current production rows have `qty_ltr` NULL; development has 129. That is a four-row difference in the same area. It affects quantity rather than `amount`, but confirm it does not touch value.

**The FY2024-25 day/month reversal** — 28,613 rows. Confirm no reversed row has leaked into an FY2026-27 month label.

**`version_status`.** Confirm both figures come from `sale_line_current`, and report how many rows in each environment are non-current for these months.

## 5. The 1,127 absent codes

Separate from the reconciliation, and worth answering while you are in the data.

**1,127 FY2026-27 sold codes are absent from the pricing catalogue, worth ₹24.76 Cr.** The earlier specification said 780 codes and ₹9.64 Cr, so that figure has moved substantially too.

Zero are catalogue-present-but-unpriced, which means every one is genuinely unknown to the pricing system rather than merely unpriced.

Report a breakdown by value, and what they are — discontinued products still selling from stock, trading goods, project or institutional items, or codes that never entered the catalogue. The largest twenty by value would be enough to characterise them.

---

## 6. What must not happen

**Do not adjust production to match the control.** Do not adjust the control to match production. Do not add a reconciling entry.

One of the two is wrong, and which one is the finding. A correction applied before the cause is understood destroys the evidence and leaves the same defect in place.
