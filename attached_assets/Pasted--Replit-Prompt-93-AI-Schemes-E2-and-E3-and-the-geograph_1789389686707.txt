not ₹8.29 Cr.**

spread in FY2025-26, 175× in FY2026-27, against my estimated 122×.

The medians are the sharper fact. **FY2025-26 SCARCE has a median of 0 retailers**
— more than half of scarce codes appear in no secondary transaction at all —
against 557 for VERY HIGH.

## Section D — the four open decisions, answered

```
Confirm these before E4 is scoped.

D1. MARGINAL PAIR DEFINITION.
    Use the DISTRIBUTION, not a point value. Show any breadth figure as a
    range with the median labelled. Rs 3,468 is a reference, not a
    forecast.

    Never present breadth arithmetic as a prediction. It states what a
    given number of new pairs would be worth at observed pair values, and
    nothing about whether they will occur.

D2. UNRESOLVED GEOGRAPHY.
    FAIL CLOSED. A retailer without an unambiguous canonical state gets no
    peer set and no proposal. Never fall back to a national or
    distributor-wide peer group - that would produce confident offers on
    the weakest evidence.

    Report the excluded population on the page: "peer targeting available
    for 2,866 of 6,194 active retailers - 46.27%."

D3. HELD-MARGIN SUPPRESSION.
    At CALCULATION time, per Section C rule 3. A proposal must never be
    generated from held data and then filtered at display - the cost of a
    near-miss is a scheme built on a figure known to be 21 points wrong.

D4. SUBSTITUTION.
    A DISCLOSED RISK, not a solved question. Every proposal names the
    nearest same-group SKUs and shows their recent movement.

    The report is right that the database cannot causally distinguish an
    addition from a switch. Do not build a model that implies it can.

E4 stays unscoped until H4 improves geography coverage materially above
46.27%.
```

## Notes

- **Section B ships value on its own.** The median retailer count per band is the
  single most useful new fact here — FY2025-26 SCARCE at **0**, VERY HIGH at 557.
  That is not a distribution difference, it is presence against absence, and it
  changes what you would do about it.
- **D2 is the one I would hold hardest.** A peer set built on incomplete geography
  would generate confident offers from the weakest evidence, and those offers cost
  money.
- **C rule 3 matters because of where the failure lands.** Filtering held data at
  display means the proposal was computed, ranked and possibly cached from a
  figure we know is wrong. Suppress at calculation.
- **H4 is a good test of the register.** It is the first entry that blocks a build
  rather than a figure, and if the register handles that cleanly it is doing its
  job.
