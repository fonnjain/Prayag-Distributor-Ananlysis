# Part 2 — Segment and code

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Segment** | highest-level grouping, from `group_canon` (curated) or `group_raw` (ERP). **"Unmapped" if both null** |
| **Item code / SKU** | the unique product identifier |
| **Net value** | `amount` — after returns and discounts. Sub Total, never Order Total |
| **SKU breadth** | distinct codes bought by a customer in a period |
| **Segment breadth** | distinct segments bought |

> **Never derive segment from `type_raw`.** It holds product groups for FY2023-24 to FY2025-26 and is **null for every FY2026-27 row** — a `type_raw` segment silently returns nothing for the live year and the page looks empty rather than broken. `group_canon` is fully populated; the Unmapped bucket is zero.

**There are 17 segments.** The FY2024-25 SALE tab's 7-group taxonomy is a separate source, not a parent.

# Part 3 — Ever Sold, the breadth denominator

> **CORRECTION 2.** The implementation doc defines Ever Sold as codes sold *"in the selected FY"*. **It must be across all loaded fiscal years.**

Single-FY would make breadth jump whenever the year selector moves, and would exclude codes that sold in FY2024-25 but not this year — **which are precisely the gap codes the page exists to surface.**

```
Ever Sold  = distinct codes sold by anyone, ALL loaded FYs, territory channel
Breadth %  = codes bought (period) ÷ Ever Sold
```

## Why not the catalogue

`item_master` cannot serve as the denominator: five segments have **zero** entries, and the `item_group` column is blank for **51%** of codes.

| Universe | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Ever Sold** | the **push** universe — demand proven somewhere |
| `item_master` − Ever Sold | the **review** universe — never sold by anyone |

## Breadth % is not a health signal

Segments range from **3 codes to 2,657**. A 3-code segment at 100% and a 2,657-code segment at 38% are not comparable. **Rank by opportunity, not breadth percentage.** The breadth bar carries no colour.

# Part 4 — Gap value

**Gap codes** = codes in Ever Sold that this scope did not buy in the period.

**Gap value** = realised sales of those codes, **in the same fiscal months across all loaded FYs.**

Three rules:

1. **Same fiscal months.** A Q1 selection sums only April–June of prior years
2. **No extrapolation.** Bottom-up from what those codes actually earned — never `unbought × mean net`, which assumes the tail sells like the head and produced a figure claiming 75% uplift on the year
3. **Project excluded** — see Part 5

---

# Part 5 — Project exclusion

**Exclude `head_canon = 'Non-territory / Project / Govt'` from every territory figure.**

For fiscal years without `head_canon`, a **Customer Bridge** applies: any customer ever attributed to the project channel in an attributed FY (23-24, 26-27) is excluded from territory views.

| Segment | Gap before | After | Project share |
|---|---|---|---|
| HDPE | ₹32.65 Cr | ₹0.01 Cr | **100%** |
| PTMT | ₹6.14 Cr | ₹1.03 Cr | **83.2%** |
| CP | ₹7.73 Cr | ₹4.61 Cr | 40.4% |
| SWR | ₹4.42 Cr | ₹4.39 Cr | 0.7% |

**Project was 6% of revenue and 78% of apparent opportunity.** A small channel making concentrated, high-value, non-repeating purchases dominates any metric built on historical value of unbought codes.

**PTMT is the one nobody was looking at.** HDPE was caught because its number was absurd; PTMT at 83% would have sent the field chasing project codes indefinitely.

# Part 6 — Peer cohorts

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Quintile** | distributors ranked by net in **FY2025-26** (the cohort FY). 1 = top 20% |
| **Membership** | same quintile **± 1** — a Q2 distributor sees Q1, Q2 and Q3 peers |
| **State cohort** | same `state_canon`, **normalised** for splits like `DELHI A` / `DELHI NCR` |
| **Territory cohort** | same State Head, when the state holds < 8 distributors |
| **National cohort** | fallback within the size band |
| **Peer count** | cohort peers buying a code the target does not |

**The cohort FY must be a COMPLETE year.** Using the current year would make the cohort circular with the period being assessed.

**Minimum 3 segment-active peers** before a code appears. One peer buying something is not evidence of demand.

**Project entities are strictly excluded from cohorts** — a project buyer at ₹5 Cr a quarter distorts quintiles for every genuine distributor in the state.

**Signals are season-filtered:** if Apr–Jun is selected, peer net and peer count reflect Apr–Jun peer activity only.

**Name the peers.** *"13 of 27 peers in this territory buy this code and you do not"* is arguable in a way a target never is.

# Part 7 — Push, Review and the four tiers

**These are opposite lists and must never merge.**

| List | Definition | Belongs to |
|---|---|---|
| **Push** | slow for this customer, **strong among peers** | the salesperson |
| **Review** | slow **everywhere** | whoever owns the catalogue |
| **Dead codes** | zero orders from anybody | the catalogue, not a target list |

## The tiers, ranked by ease of execution

| Tier | Definition | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Range** | fills a gap in an `item_group` the distributor already buys | shelf space and relationship exist |
| **2 — Lapsed** | bought in the cohort FY, not in the current period | proven demand from **this** customer |
| **3 — Active** | a segment where the distributor is currently active | one step out |
| **4 — New** | a segment with zero current purchases | hardest |

For one distributor this reduced **234 codes to 108, with 90% in tiers 1 and 2.** Only 7 asked them to enter a new category.

> **67% being range depth suggests systematic range work is not happening in the field** — and it is the cheapest thing to act on.

Within a tier, rank by **peer penetration × median peer net**. Default the card to the top 10.

**Tier 1 resolution depends on `item_group`**, which is blank for 51% of codes. Those fall cleanly to tiers 3 and 4 — no false signals, just less resolution.

# Part 8 — Discount

**Two measures. Never in the same column.**

| Measure | Formula | Availability |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary MRP discount** | `1 − (net ÷ (mrp × qty))`, weighted `Σ(disc × net) ÷ Σ(net)` | all four years |
| **Secondary register discount** | the `discount_pct` column, a **percentage** | closed years only |

## The population gate

The register measure is shown **only if ≥ 99% of source lines satisfy** `|gross × (1 − disc%/100) − net| < 1`. If it fails, **the measure is blocked**, not approximated.

**It currently passes on 100.00% of 379,439 FY2025-26 lines.** Discount is a percentage, not an amount — subtracting it as rupees would understate Sub Total on every line with no error shown.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Norm discount** | territory-wide average for a code across closed FYs |
| **Above-norm flag** | current discount **≥ 5 points** above its norm — a **margin question** |
| **Anomalous** | `discount_pct > 100`. Excluded from weighted averages, flagged |

> **The variance is the finding, not the average.** One distributor materially below another on the same code is an immediate commercial conversation.

**MRP discount is explicitly NOT margin.** Never substitute it.

# Part 9 — Seasonality

> **CORRECTION 3.** The implementation doc computes seasonality over **all channels**. That is correct for the market-pattern view, but **banding and ranking must use territory-only curves.**

The all-channel view put HDPE at the top of the push list on a Q1 peak that was **100% project**:

| Channel | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | 3-yr net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Project | 40.9% | 28.8% | 24.6% | 5.7% | ₹79.7 Cr |
| **Territory** | **0.2%** | 43.7% | **47.6%** | 8.5% | **₹8.3 Cr** |

**Territory HDPE Q1 was ₹2 lakh across three years.** On the territory curve it peaks Q3 — genuinely in season in Q2, for reasons unrelated to why it appeared to be.

**Keep the all-channel figure on the Timing tab, labelled as a market-wide pattern. Band and rank on territory.**

## The pattern

**13 of 14 segments peak Q4 (Jan–Mar).** The skew is mild — 28–35% against a flat 25% — so the catalogue sells year-round, just best in Q4.

| Segment | Peak | Share | Consistency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Garden Pipe | Q4 | **43.3%** | 3/3 |
| UPVC | Q4 | 37.6% | 3/3 |
| CPVC | Q4 | 35.5% | **2/3** |
| CP | Q4 | 32.3% | 3/3 |
| PTMT | Q4 | 30.9% | 3/3 |

**Consistency** — the peak falling in the same quarter across all three closed FYs — is what makes it a pattern rather than an artefact. **2/3 is a weaker claim and should say so on the badge.**

## Banding

Cards band by **current-quarter share of the year**, not peak quarter alone — otherwise everything reads "groundwork" in August.

The header states the basis. **"August work on Q4 codes is January groundwork, not a miss"** — which protects the field from being measured on a season five months away.

**Thin history:** cards under ₹3 Cr territory history carry an explicit warning.

# Part 10 — Trends

| Tracker | Definition |
|---|---|
| **First order** | a `(customer, code)` pair with no history in any prior FY |
| **Lost code** | bought by an active customer in the prior FY, missing in the current |
| **Breadth narrower** | distinct code count dropped versus prior FY |
| **Dropped value** | Σ prior-FY net for codes not bought this year, by a **still-active** customer |

**Rank narrowers by value, not code count** — losing 5 high-value codes beats losing 20 marginal ones.

# Part 12 — Common misreadings

**"Breadth 38% is worse than 100%."** Not comparable — 1,000 of 2,657 codes versus 3 of 3.

**"This is a ₹32 Cr opportunity."** Check the channel. HDPE's was 100% project, from five contractors who have all exited.

**"Slow-moving means push it."** Only if peers buy it. Slow everywhere is a catalogue question.

**"HDPE peaks in Q1."** On the all-channel curve, yes — and it is entirely project. Territory HDPE peaks Q3.

**"Nothing is in season, so August is wasted."** The catalogue is mildly Q4-skewed; August work is legitimate January groundwork.

**"The gap value is what we would earn."** It is what those codes **have** earned. Evidence of demand, not a forecast.
