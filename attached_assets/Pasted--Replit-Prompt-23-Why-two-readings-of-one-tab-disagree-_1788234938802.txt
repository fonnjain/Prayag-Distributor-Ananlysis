# Replit Prompt 23 — Why two readings of one tab disagree

**Date:** 1 September 2026
**Follows:** Prompt 20
**Scope:** Read-only. No code change, no snapshot rebuild, no reload, no backfill.

**Standing rule:** every figure states which database or source it came from.

**Why this exists:** Prompt 20 established that `deep_dive_snapshot.orderBooking`
and `secondary_head_month.ordered_amount` disagree for 141 of 189 members,
₹10.51 crore absolute. Both are read from the same workbook. This asks why.

I tested the obvious explanation and it does not hold. Every member with monthly
rows has exactly 5 non-null months, so a clean period offset would produce a
uniform gap. Instead the share of each member's monthly total that the snapshot
fails to reproduce has a **median of 7.5%, quartiles of 2.7% and 24.1%, and a
range from 0% to 100%**. Fifteen members reconcile to the rupee; Amruth Banakar
is out by 100%. There is no single mechanism producing that spread.

## Notes

- Section A2 is the whole prompt in one question. Six members' raw cells, side by
  side, should show immediately whether the cumulative column is a formula, a
  paste, or hand-typed. If it is hand-typed by team members, the two figures were
  never going to agree, the ₹10.51 crore is not a defect, and the question
  becomes which one anyone should be looking at.
- B4 collapses two open roster threads into one row. Narendra Kumar Sharma —
  active state head, absent from `heads_resources` — appears as a member under
  Sunil Mohanty, who has no organisation record anywhere. That relationship comes
  from somewhere in the sheet and it should be printed rather than inferred.
- D3 is the one with teeth. A populated current year compared against an empty
  prior year produces a movement that looks like growth and is an artefact.
- E4 exists because the natural instinct is to backfill the monthly table from
  the snapshot, or rebuild the snapshot from monthly. Either would erase the
  evidence of which reading was correct.
