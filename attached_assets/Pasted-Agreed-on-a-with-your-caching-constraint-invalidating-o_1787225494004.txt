The distinction is the right one: an approved coverage DECISION and the latest
transactional VALUE attached to it are different things, and persisting the

Build it. Four things to get precise.

1. DEFINE "STRUCTURAL" IN CODE, NOT IN PROSE
Open-FY drift reports ONLY:
   - the head attributed to a customer changed
   - a customer appeared or disappeared from a leaf
   - the customer count changed
   - the effective-date range changed
   - a leaf gained or lost a head

It does NOT report a net-value difference where head, customer set, count and
dates are all unchanged.

Write that as an explicit predicate with a name, not as a filter buried in a
query. Someone will need to change it later and should find it in one place.

2. CLOSED FY STAYS STRICT
A frozen year reports ANY difference, including value. That is the whole point
of freezing - if a closed year's evidence moves, something is wrong and it
should be loud.

Confirm the boundary is read from the same freeze state the rest of the system
uses, not a separate date calculation.

3. THE CURRENT FIVE SHOULD DISAPPEAR
After the change, confirm the drift surface reports zero items. If any of the
five survives, the predicate is wrong.

4. PROVE IT STILL CATCHES THE REAL THING
Construct a structural change in a scratch copy - move one customer from one
head to another in the open FY - and confirm drift reports it. A surface that
suppresses everything is as useless as one that reports everything.

VERIFY
  a. The predicate, printed.
  b. Drift count before and after - expect 5 to 0.
  c. The synthetic structural change caught, with the item as rendered.
  d. Closed-FY behaviour unchanged: FY2025-26 still reports any difference.
  e. Read time for the open-FY derivation, and confirm the cache invalidates
     on register sync rather than expiring.
  f. Push, print both refs.
