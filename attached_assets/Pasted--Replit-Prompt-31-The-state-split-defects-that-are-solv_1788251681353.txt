# Replit Prompt 31 — The state-split defects that are solvable now

**Date:** 1 September 2026
**Scope:** Section A is read-only and must come back first. Section B is a narrow
code change. Section C is scoping only.

**Standing rule:** every figure states which database or source it came from.

**The key fact:** `state_hierarchy` (migration 025, extended by 055) is already
the authority. It has `state_canon`, `state_parent`, `is_split`,
`picker_visible`, a foreign key from `person_state_coverage` and
`customer_assignment`, and a trigger that rejects non-assignable leaves.

Seven states are correctly registered as split — Delhi, Jammu and Kashmir, Uttar
Pradesh, Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu. **Maharashtra is
registered `is_split = false` with no children**, which is why it behaves
differently from all of them.

Crucially, `state_hierarchy` registers **`UP ( A )` with spaces**, matching the
data. `territoryResolver.ts` uses `"UP (A)"` without. The authority already says
which is right.

## Notes

- A4 is the one with money attached. Scheme qualification is running on a key
  that may never match, and the correction in B1 will change who qualifies. That
  is why B asks for the before-and-after count *before* applying.
- A5 makes B3 possible. Two files disagree about east versus west, and the
  coverage data can settle it without asking anyone.
- Section C is deliberately scoping only. C2 in particular — if the source
  workbooks no longer carry the raw labels, Maharashtra's history is
  unrecoverable and that should be recorded as a fact rather than discovered
  later by someone expecting to fix it.
